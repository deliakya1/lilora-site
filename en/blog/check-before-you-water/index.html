Why Lilora's reminders say "check", not "water"

A calendar cannot see your plant. Why Lilora's reminders ask for a check and keep "Watered" and "Checked, no water needed" apart.

A short answer

A calendar cannot see your plant. The same plant can need water after five days in a warm, bright July and stay damp for two weeks in a dim January. So Lilora's reminders ask you to check the plant, and you record what you actually did: "Watered", or "Checked, no water needed". That small change in wording follows what horticultural advisers have said for years: water when the mix needs it, not when the date comes round. University of Maryland Extension: watering indoor plants

The problem with "water every Sunday"

Fixed schedules are attractive because they are easy to remember. But how fast a pot dries depends on the plant, the size and material of the pot, the mix, the light, the temperature and the season. The RHS notes, for example, that pots dry out faster when central heating lowers humidity, and that plants in cooler rooms need watering less often. RHS: how to grow houseplants

A schedule that fits one month will be wrong in another. And the usual direction of the mistake is too much water: overwatering is one of the most common causes of houseplant trouble, because roots in constantly wet mix lack air and can die. University of Maryland Extension: overwatered indoor plants

What a check is

A check takes less than a minute:

  1. Push a finger into the mix below the surface, or use a wooden skewer in a deep pot.
  2. Lift the pot and compare its weight with how it feels after a full watering.
  3. Look at the plant: firm or limp leaves, new growth, anything unusual.
  4. Decide: water thoroughly and let it drain, or leave it for now.

University of Maryland Extension describes both the touch test and the weight test; as a plant uses water, the mix dries and the pot becomes lighter. University of Maryland Extension: watering indoor plants When you do water, water until the excess drains and then empty the saucer. Illinois Extension: watering

Our longer guide, how to water houseplants without a rigid schedule, goes through this step by step, and overwatered or underwatered? helps when a plant already looks unhappy.

How this shows up in the app

The labels are the ones in the app's English interface. In Turkish they read "Suladım" and "Kontrol ettim, suya ihtiyacı yok". Snoozing is deliberately not a care record: it moves the reminder, but nobody has looked at the plant yet.

Why two records are better than one

If the only button were "Watered", a check that ended with no water would leave no trace, and the history would suggest the plant had been neglected. Keeping the two apart gives you a truer picture:

  • A run of "Checked, no water needed" in winter is normal, not a failure.
  • If a plant keeps needing water at every check, the interval or the pot may be worth a look.
  • If a pot stays wet check after check while the plant declines, it is time to look at drainage and roots; see root rot in houseplants.

In a shared household this matters even more. Whoever checks next can see that someone already looked this morning and decided the mix was still moist. Our guide to sharing plant care covers that side.

What a reminder cannot do

A reminder is a prompt, not a diagnosis. It does not know your room temperature, your window or the day you repotted. Reminders in Lilora arrive at 9:00 local time on the day they are due. There is also an optional watering reminder for people who want one, and the app says plainly next to it: "Lilora cannot tell whether a plant needs water; check the soil first." The decision about water stays with the person looking at the plant. Lilora is currently in App Store review, so this describes the app as submitted.

Common questions

Doesn't checking take longer than just watering?

Only slightly, and it avoids the most common mistake. With practice, lifting a pot tells you most of what you need.

What if I'm away?

Leave the next check to someone else and make sure they know how to read the mix. The RHS has advice on holiday care. RHS: holiday care

Can I still set a short interval for thirsty plants?

Yes. A short check interval suits plants that dry fast. The point is that each reminder ends with a decision, not automatic water.
