Spider plant care

Chlorophytum comosum

Published by Lilora 22 October 2026 · Reviewed 22 October 2026

Care level
Easy

Spider plant is a forgiving, fast-growing houseplant from tropical and southern Africa. Give it medium light without direct sun, keep the mix lightly moist in growth, and use rain or filtered water if tap water browns the leaf tips. It produces plantlets on long stems that root easily. The ASPCA lists it as non-toxic to cats and dogs. NC State Extension

Light

Spider plant prefers medium light and tolerates shade, but it does not like direct sunlight. A spot a little back from a bright window, or a well-lit shelf, suits it. Variegated forms keep their stripes better with good indirect light. NC State Extension

Read more in the indoor light guide.

How to check before watering

It grows best in moist soil and tolerates short dry spells. During growth, water when the surface of the mix has dried; in winter, reduce watering. Thick white roots store water, so a slightly dry pot is less risky than a constantly wet one. NC State Extension

The watering guide explains how to combine touch and pot weight.

Potting mix and pot

A loam-based or general houseplant mix with good drainage suits it. Fleshy roots can fill a pot quickly and even lift the plant; repot or divide when roots crowd the surface.

Humidity and temperature

Medium humidity is enough; a humidifier or pebble tray helps in very dry air. Keep it above 7 °C (45 °F). NC State Extension

Feeding

Feed lightly during spring and summer. NC State warns that over-fertilizing reduces plantlet formation. NC State Extension

Seasonal care

NC State advises reducing watering in winter and keeping the plant above 7 °C (45 °F). It grows best in evenly moist mix but tolerates dry spells, so in cool months check before watering and let the top dry a little more. NC State Extension

Short days have a useful side effect: NC State notes that runners form when the plant gets less than 12 hours of light a day for at least three weeks, so plantlets often appear after autumn. Keep it out of direct sun, which it does not tolerate, in summer. See the winter care guide. In Lilora, a reminder is a prompt to check, not an order to water: you record whether you watered or snoozed, so slower drying in the cold months shows up in your own history.

Common problems

  • Brown leaf tips: NC State links them to chlorine and fluoride in tap water. Try rainwater or filtered water and avoid fertilizer build-up.
  • Pale, limp leaves with wet mix: check drainage.
  • No plantlets: plants often make runners after several weeks of short days and long uninterrupted nights; heavy feeding also reduces them.

See the yellow-leaf checks for broader troubleshooting.

Propagation

Spider plant propagates easily from plantlets or by division. Pin a plantlet onto moist mix while it is still attached, or root it in water, then cut the runner once roots form. NC State notes that runners form when the plant receives less than 12 hours of light a day for at least three weeks. NC State Extension

Pets and people

The ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center lists spider plant as non-toxic to cats and dogs, and NC State Extension tags it the same way. Non-toxic does not mean a pet can eat it freely: chewing plant material can still upset a stomach, and cats are often drawn to the dangling leaves. Hang it out of reach if your cat chews it. If a pet seems unwell, contact a veterinarian. For people in Türkiye, the poison information line is 114 (UZEM). This is general information, not veterinary or medical advice. To compare other plants, see the pet-safe houseplants guide or the non-toxic profiles in the plant library.

Common questions

Why does my spider plant have brown tips?

Tap-water chemicals, dry air and fertilizer build-up are common causes. Change one thing at a time.

When should I cut off the plantlets?

Once they have roots of a few centimetres, or leave them on for a fuller hanging plant.

Is spider plant safe for cats?

The ASPCA lists it as non-toxic to cats and dogs. Still discourage chewing.

Why hasn’t my spider plant made plantlets?

Runners usually form after several weeks of days with less than 12 hours of light. Heavy feeding also reduces them, according to NC State.
