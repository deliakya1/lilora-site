# Peace lily care

Peace lilies are shade-loving aroids from tropical America and the western Pacific. Keep them out of direct sun, keep the mix moist but never soggy, give them warmth and some humidity, and feed lightly. A drooping peace lily is a reason to check the mix, not automatically to water. The ASPCA lists it as toxic to cats and dogs. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/spathiphyllum/)

## Light

Peace lilies prefer partial to deep shade; NC State says not to place them in direct sun. The RHS suggests a bright spot in partial shade and advises avoiding sunny windowsills. Flowering is usually better in a brighter position than in a dark corner. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/spathiphyllum/) · [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/getmedia/d02059a1-4e2c-45c3-956b-da5cc90db7f1/Indoor-plants-resources.pdf)

See the [indoor light guide](/en/guides/indoor-plant-light-guide/) to tell shade from a truly dark spot.

## How to check before watering

Keep the mix moist but not soggy, and let it dry slightly between waterings. Check often, because peace lilies dry faster than succulents. When the top of the mix feels just dry and the pot lighter, water thoroughly or bottom-water, which NC State says works well, then drain completely. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/spathiphyllum/)

Peace lilies wilt visibly when dry, but wet roots can also cause drooping. Feel the mix before you add water. The [watering guide](/en/guides/watering-houseplants/) explains how.

## Potting mix and pot

NC State recommends a potting mix high in organic matter, slightly acidic and well drained. Repot when roots fill the pot; NC State suggests late winter. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/spathiphyllum/)

## Humidity and temperature

The ideal daytime range is about 20–29 °C (68–85 °F), with no more than about a 5–6 °C (10 °F) drop at night. Between about 4 and 16 °C (40–60 °F) growth slows considerably, and long periods below 4 °C (40 °F) can damage the plant. The RHS notes that peace lilies prefer humid air and may suffer in very dry rooms. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/spathiphyllum/) · [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/getmedia/d02059a1-4e2c-45c3-956b-da5cc90db7f1/Indoor-plants-resources.pdf)

## Feeding

Sources differ in detail. NC State advises feeding at a quarter of the recommended strength and flushing the mix between feeds; the RHS suggests a balanced liquid fertilizer each month at the packet rate. The shared message is to feed gently in active growth and avoid salt build-up, which can burn leaf tips. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/spathiphyllum/)

## Seasonal care

NC State notes that cold, draughty conditions of about 4–16 °C (40–60 °F) slow growth considerably, and long spells below 4 °C (40 °F) can damage leaves, stems and roots. In winter, keep the plant in a warm room, away from cold windows and doors, and keep checking the mix rather than watering on a fixed day; slower growth usually means slower drying. NC State suggests repotting, when needed, in February or March. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/spathiphyllum/)

Heated rooms are often dry, and NC State links low humidity to brown leaf tips. In summer, keep it out of direct sun, which also browns the tips. See the [winter care guide](/en/guides/winter-houseplant-care/) and the [humidity guide](/en/guides/houseplant-humidity/). In Lilora, a reminder is a prompt to check, not an order to water: you record whether you watered or snoozed, so slower drying in the cold months shows up in your own history.

## Common problems

- **Brown leaf tips:** NC State lists direct sun, underwatering, low humidity and fertilizer salts as possible causes.
- **Mealybugs:** the most common indoor pest; wipe leaves with a damp cloth.
- **No flowers:** often too little light.

The [yellow-leaf checks](/en/guides/yellow-houseplant-leaves/) cover other leaf changes.

## Propagation

Divide a crowded clump when repotting, keeping roots and several leaves on each division. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/spathiphyllum/)

## Pets and people

The [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center](https://www.aspca.org/) lists peace lily as toxic to cats and dogs because of insoluble calcium oxalates. It lists oral irritation, burning and irritation of the mouth, tongue and lips, excessive drooling, vomiting and difficulty swallowing. Despite the common name, it is not a true lily (*Lilium*). If a pet chews it, contact a veterinarian or an animal poison control service. For people in Türkiye, the poison information line is 114 (UZEM). This is general information, not veterinary or medical advice. To compare other plants, see the [pet-safe houseplants guide](/en/guides/pet-safe-houseplants/) or the [non-toxic profiles in the plant library](/en/plants/#petNonToxic).

## Common questions

### Why is my peace lily drooping?

It may be dry, but soggy roots can also cause drooping. Check the mix first.

### Is the white “flower” a petal?

It is a spathe, a modified leaf around the flower spike.

### Can it grow in low light?

It tolerates shade, but flowering is usually better with more indirect light.

### When should I repot a peace lily?

When roots fill the pot. NC State suggests February or March, moving up only modestly in size.
