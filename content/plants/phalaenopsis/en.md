# Moth orchid care

Moth orchids (*Phalaenopsis*) grow on trees in tropical and subtropical Asia and north-east Australia. Indoors they need bright indirect light, a bark-based orchid mix, watering that drains completely, warmth and good humidity. A cooler period helps many plants produce a new flower spike. The ASPCA lists them as non-toxic to cats and dogs. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/phalaenopsis/)

## Light

Give bright, indirect light. NC State suggests an east windowsill, or a well-shaded west or south window. The RHS advises bright light in winter to encourage flowering and keeping plants out of direct summer sun, which can scorch leaves. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/phalaenopsis/) · [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/phalaenopsis/growing-guide)

Dark green, floppy leaves suggest too little light; yellowish or scorched leaves can mean too much. See the [indoor light guide](/en/guides/indoor-plant-light-guide/).

## How to check before watering

Water when the bark is starting to dry. Roots that look silvery through a clear pot and a lighter pot are useful clues; green, plump roots have recently been watered. Water in the morning with tepid water, preferably rainwater, either from above or by standing the pot in a bowl, then drain thoroughly. Never leave it standing in water, and never let the roots dry out completely. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/phalaenopsis/) · [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/phalaenopsis/growing-guide)

Mature plants may need water about weekly in the growing season and less in winter, but let the bark tell you. The [watering guide](/en/guides/watering-houseplants/) explains why.

## Potting mix and pot

Use a specially formulated, bark-based orchid mix. The RHS warns that loam-based or multipurpose compost will kill moth orchids. Repot every couple of years, or when the bark breaks down; healthy roots are plump, firm and silvery-white. [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/phalaenopsis/growing-guide)

## Humidity and temperature

The RHS gives an ideal range of 19–30 °C (66–86 °F) by day and 16–19 °C (61–66 °F) at night, with a minimum of 16 °C (60 °F). Keep plants away from heaters and draughts. They prefer high humidity; a wide saucer of damp gravel under the pot helps. [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/phalaenopsis/growing-guide)

## Feeding

Feed lightly and regularly in the growing season with an orchid fertilizer, watering without feed every fourth time to flush salts, and feed sparingly, if at all, in winter. [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/phalaenopsis/growing-guide)

## Common problems

- **No new flowers:** NC State notes that many large-flowered hybrids need nights around 13 °C (55 °F) for about a week in autumn; the RHS suggests a spot about 5 °C cooler for a month.
- **Bud drop:** sudden changes in temperature, moisture or position.
- **Shrivelled leaves:** too little water, or roots damaged by soggy mix. Check the roots.
- **Pests:** mealybugs and scale.

## Propagation

Home propagation is uncommon. Some plants form a small plantlet (keiki) on the flower stem, which can be potted once it has roots a few centimetres long.

After flowering, the RHS suggests cutting the old stalk just above the second node, or at the base if it has dried. [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/phalaenopsis/growing-guide)

## Pets and people

The [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center](https://www.aspca.org/) lists *Phalaenopsis* orchids as non-toxic to cats and dogs, and NC State Extension tags them the same way. Non-toxic does not mean a pet can eat it freely: chewing can still upset a stomach, and treated or freshly fertilized plants carry other risks. If a pet seems unwell, contact a veterinarian. For people in Türkiye, the poison information line is 114 (UZEM). This is general information, not veterinary or medical advice.

## Common questions

### Should I water orchids with ice cubes?

The sources here recommend tepid water and thorough drainage instead.

### Why are the roots growing out of the pot?

Aerial roots are normal for this epiphyte. Leave healthy ones in place.

### Should I cut the stem after flowering?

Cut just above the second node, or at the base if the stem has turned pale and dry.
