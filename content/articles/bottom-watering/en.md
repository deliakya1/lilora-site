# Bottom watering houseplants: when it helps and when it doesn't

Bottom watering means standing the pot in a saucer or bowl of water so the mix soaks it up through the drainage holes. It is handy for plants whose leaves should stay dry, such as African violets, and for mix that has dried out so much that water runs straight through. Lift the pot out once the surface feels moist, usually after 20–30 minutes, and never leave it standing in water. Because no water flushes through, salts can build up, so water from the top now and then to rinse them out. [University of Maryland Extension: watering indoor plants](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/watering-indoor-plants)

## How to do it

1. Check that the plant actually needs water: push a finger into the mix and lift the pot. Our guide to [watering houseplants without a rigid schedule](/en/guides/watering-houseplants/) explains the check.
2. Fill a saucer, bowl or sink with a few centimetres of room-temperature water.
3. Stand the pot in it. The pot must have a drainage hole.
4. Wait until the surface of the mix feels damp. University of Wisconsin–Madison Extension suggests 20 to 30 minutes for African violets before removing any excess water. [University of Wisconsin–Madison Extension: houseplant care](https://hort.extension.wisc.edu/articles/houseplant-care/)
5. Lift the pot out, let it drain and pour away what is left.

The University of Maryland describes the same method: water is absorbed and drawn up into the mix, and leftover water is poured out. [University of Maryland Extension: watering indoor plants](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/watering-indoor-plants)

## When bottom watering helps

- **Plants that dislike wet leaves.** Iowa State notes that African violets are often watered this way, because water left on the leaves, especially cold water, causes discoloured spots. [Iowa State University Extension: all about African violets](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/all-about-african-violets)
- **Very dry, shrunken mix.** When peat-based mix dries out completely, it can pull away from the pot so water poured on top runs down the sides. A soak from below rewets it evenly.
- **Rosette plants** whose crowns rot if water sits in the centre.

## The drawbacks

Colorado State University Extension says that, generally speaking, it is better to water from the top, because subirrigation can lead to soluble salts accumulating in and on the surface of the mix. High salt levels reduce water uptake and root growth. [Colorado State University Extension: watering houseplants](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/houseplants/1318-houseplants-watering/) The University of Maryland also notes that fertiliser salts can collect as a white crust on the surface, especially on bottom-watered plants. [University of Maryland Extension: fertilizer toxicity or high soluble salts](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/fertilizer-toxicity-or-high-soluble-salts-indoor-plants)

The fix is simple: if you mostly water from below, water thoroughly from the top every so often and let it drain, so the excess salts wash out. [Colorado State University Extension: watering houseplants](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/houseplants/1318-houseplants-watering/) Our post on [tap water for houseplants](/en/blog/tap-water-for-houseplants/) covers how often to flush.

## Don't leave it soaking

The commonest mistake is forgetting the pot in the water. Iowa State warns that plants should not stay in saucers of water for long periods, as they may rot, and that the top 2–3 cm of mix should dry before the next soak. [Iowa State University Extension: all about African violets](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/all-about-african-violets) Constantly wet roots run short of air; see [root rot in houseplants](/en/guides/root-rot/) if a plant wilts in wet mix.

## Combining both methods

A practical routine for plants you usually water from below:

- Bottom water when the top of the mix is dry, and lift the pot out once the surface is damp.
- Every few months, take the plant to the sink and water from the top until water runs out of the bottom. The University of Maryland suggests using at least twice the pot's volume and leaching every four to six months. [University of Maryland Extension: watering indoor plants](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/watering-indoor-plants)
- Flush before you feed, so fertiliser does not land on top of old salts. [University of Maryland Extension: watering indoor plants](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/watering-indoor-plants)
- If a white crust appears on the surface, scrape it off and flush sooner.

## Common questions

### Is bottom watering better than top watering?

Not in general. Top watering is simpler and flushes salts. Bottom watering is a useful tool for specific plants and situations.

### How do I know the plant has taken up enough?

The surface of the mix turns darker and feels damp, and the pot feels noticeably heavier when you lift it.

### Can I bottom water several plants in one tray?

You can, but if one plant has pests or disease, shared water can spread problems. Use a separate container for any plant you are unsure about.
