# Grow lights for houseplants: a simple starting set-up

A grow light helps when a room is too dark for the plants you want to keep, or when winter days are short. You do not need a specialist rig: an LED fixture with broad-spectrum ("daylight" or "full spectrum") light, hung about 30–60 cm above the leaves and run on a timer for roughly 12–14 hours a day, is a sensible starting point. Then watch the plants for a few weeks and adjust the distance or hours. Plants also need a proper dark period every night. [Iowa State University Extension: setting up supplemental lights](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/how-set-supplemental-lights-indoor-plants)

## Does your plant need one?

Signs that light is the limiting factor include slow, weak growth, long gaps between leaves and stems leaning hard towards the window. The RHS also lists poor growth with dark green foliage as a sign that plants under lamps need more light. [RHS: artificial lighting for indoor plants](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/types/houseplants/artificial-lighting) Before buying anything, try the brightest window you have; our [indoor plant light guide](/en/guides/indoor-plant-light-guide/) explains how to judge it, and [leggy houseplants](/en/blog/leggy-houseplants/) covers stretched growth.

## Which kind of light

The University of Florida notes that plants can grow under artificial light alone if the source is suitable. Incandescent bulbs work but are inefficient and hot; fluorescent and LED lights are better, and LEDs use less electricity for the same light output. Broad-spectrum light supports better growth. [UF/IFAS Gardening Solutions: light for houseplants](https://gardeningsolutions.ifas.ufl.edu/plants/houseplants/light-for-houseplants/)

The RHS explains that plants mainly use blue and red light: blue supports leafy growth and red supports growth and flowering. Too much blue with little red tends to give compact, dark plants with few flowers, while too much red or far-red gives tall, spindly growth. [RHS: artificial lighting for indoor plants](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/types/houseplants/artificial-lighting) For foliage plants at home, a white, broad-spectrum LED is the simplest choice and looks normal in a living room.

Iowa State's starting point for an LED shop-style fixture is full-spectrum light of at least 3,000 lumens. [Iowa State University Extension: setting up supplemental lights](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/how-set-supplemental-lights-indoor-plants) A small clip-on lamp gives far less and suits only a single plant close to the bulb.

Clemson's Home & Garden Information Center says 16 hours of light and 8 of darkness suits most plants when making up for weak light. [RHS: artificial lighting for indoor plants](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/types/houseplants/artificial-lighting)

## Use a timer, and keep the nights dark

A cheap plug-in timer does the remembering for you, so the lights switch on and off at the same time every day. Iowa State recommends plugging all supplemental lights into a timer, and stresses that plants need a dark period to grow properly; some need it to flower. Other light sources, such as room lamps or street lights, can disturb that dark period. [Iowa State University Extension: setting up supplemental lights](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/how-set-supplemental-lights-indoor-plants)

## Adjust what else changes

More light means faster growth, and faster growth means the plant uses more water and, in the growing season, more food. Check the mix more often in the first weeks under a lamp; [how to water houseplants without a rigid schedule](/en/guides/watering-houseplants/) explains the check. If you move a plant from a dim corner to a strong lamp, increase the time gradually over a week or two.

## Common questions

### Can a normal LED bulb work?

A bright, broad-spectrum household LED close to a plant can help, and the University of Florida notes that cool white, warm white and daylight lamps may all work. [UF/IFAS Gardening Solutions: light for houseplants](https://gardeningsolutions.ifas.ufl.edu/plants/houseplants/light-for-houseplants/) Output, distance and hours matter more than the label.

### Is purple light better?

Not for a home. Red-and-blue fixtures were designed for efficiency, but white light shows the plant's true colours and is easier to live with.

### Should the light stay on all night?

No. Plants need a daily dark period. Use a timer and aim for no more than 16 hours of light.
