# Brown leaf tips on houseplants: what they usually mean

Brown, crispy tips and edges are one of the most common houseplant complaints, and they rarely have a single cause. The usual suspects are dry indoor air, uneven watering, a build-up of fertiliser salts, and, for a few sensitive plants, chemicals in tap water. Trimming the brown part tidies the leaf, but new tips will brown too unless you find and change what caused it. [Iowa State University Extension: brown leaf tips and edges](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/faq/why-does-my-houseplant-have-brown-leaf-tips-and-edges)

## Start with the pattern, not the leaf

Before changing anything, look at the whole plant for a minute:

- Are the brown tips on old leaves, new leaves or both?
- Is the browning dry and papery, or soft and dark?
- Is there a white or grey crust on the potting mix or around the rim of the pot?
- Has anything changed lately: a radiator switched on, a new spot, a new fertiliser, a different water?

Soft, dark browning together with a pot that stays wet points towards the roots rather than the air; our guide to [root rot in houseplants](/en/guides/root-rot/) covers that case. The rest of this post is about the dry, crispy kind.

## Dry air and heating

Iowa State University Extension calls low humidity the most likely cause, especially in winter when heating dries indoor air. [Iowa State University Extension: brown leaf tips and edges](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/faq/why-does-my-houseplant-have-brown-leaf-tips-and-edges) Clemson's Home & Garden Information Center also links brown tips and margins to hot, dry air. [Clemson HGIC: houseplant diseases and disorders](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/houseplant-diseases-disorders/)

The practical fixes are simple: move plants away from radiators and heating vents, group them together, and consider a humidifier for thin-leaved plants. Misting feels helpful but changes very little; our guide to [humidity for houseplants](/en/guides/houseplant-humidity/) explains why.

## Uneven watering

Letting a plant dry out too far between waterings also browns tips and edges. [Iowa State University Extension: brown leaf tips and edges](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/faq/why-does-my-houseplant-have-brown-leaf-tips-and-edges) This often happens with a long gap followed by a big drink, or with small pots on a sunny sill that dry out in a day or two. Check the mix with a finger and lift the pot to feel its weight; [how to water houseplants without a rigid schedule](/en/guides/watering-houseplants/) walks through the routine.

## Salt build-up from fertiliser and hard water

Fertiliser and some tap water leave dissolved salts behind as the mix dries. Over time these salts can damage root tips, and the typical symptom is browning or dieback of leaf tips and margins. [University of Maryland Extension: fertilizer toxicity or high soluble salts](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/fertilizer-toxicity-or-high-soluble-salts-indoor-plants) A whitish crust on the mix or the rim of the pot is the visible clue. [Colorado State University Extension: leaching salts from potting mixes](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/houseplants/1339-leaching-salts-potting-mixes/)

To flush salts, Colorado State suggests removing the crust and the top 1–2 cm of mix, then filling the pot to the rim with tepid water in a sink and letting it drain completely, a couple of times over. [Colorado State University Extension: leaching salts from potting mixes](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/houseplants/1339-leaching-salts-potting-mixes/) Then feed less, or more diluted; see [feeding houseplants](/en/guides/fertilizing-houseplants/).

## Water-sensitive plants

Some plants react to chlorine, fluoride and other chemicals in tap water over time. Iowa State names spider plant, ti plant, dracaena, prayer plant and calathea as examples. [Iowa State University Extension: brown leaf tips and edges](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/faq/why-does-my-houseplant-have-brown-leaf-tips-and-edges) If one of these keeps browning while your other plants are fine, try a different water source for a few months. Our post on [tap water for houseplants](/en/blog/tap-water-for-houseplants/) goes into the options.

## Common questions

### Should I cut off the brown tips?

You can. Use clean, sharp scissors and follow the leaf's natural shape, leaving a thin line of brown so you do not cut into healthy tissue. It is cosmetic only.

### Will the brown parts turn green again?

No. Damaged tissue stays brown. Judge your changes by the new leaves that grow over the next month or two.

### Is one brown tip a reason to worry?

Usually not. An occasional brown tip on an old leaf is normal. Look for a pattern across many leaves before you change the plant's care.
