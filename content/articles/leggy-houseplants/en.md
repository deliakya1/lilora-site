# Leggy houseplants: why they stretch and how to fix them

A leggy plant has long, thin stems with wide gaps between the leaves, often leaning towards the window. The usual cause is not enough light: the plant stretches to reach more of it. Stretched stems do not shrink back, so the fix has two parts. First, give the plant more light so new growth stays compact. Then, ideally in early spring, cut the long stems back just above a leaf node to encourage bushier growth. The cuttings can often be rooted. [University of Minnesota Extension: spring houseplant care](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/houseplants/spring-houseplant-care)

## How to recognise it

Typical signs of stretching:

- Long gaps between leaves on new growth compared with older growth.
- Smaller, paler new leaves.
- The whole plant leaning or growing towards the light.
- A bare lower stem with leaves only at the tip.

The University of Minnesota notes that many houseplants stretch towards limited winter light, so a slightly lanky look by the end of winter is common. [University of Minnesota Extension: spring houseplant care](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/houseplants/spring-houseplant-care) A plant that is old and bare at the base may simply be ageing, but the remedy is similar.

## Step one: more light

Pruning alone will not help if the plant stays in the same dim spot; the new shoots will stretch again. Move it closer to a window, or to a brighter one. If you are moving a plant into much stronger light, do it gradually over one to two weeks so the leaves do not scorch. [University of Minnesota Extension: spring houseplant care](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/houseplants/spring-houseplant-care) Our [indoor plant light guide](/guides/indoor-plant-light-guide/) explains how to read the light in a room.

If there is no brighter spot, a grow light can make up the difference. Clemson University's Home & Garden Information Center notes that extending the light period can compensate for weak light, and that 16 hours of light and 8 of darkness suits most plants. [Clemson HGIC: indoor plants, light requirements](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/indoor-plants-cleaning-fertilizing-containers-light-requirements/) Our post on [grow lights for houseplants](/blog/grow-lights-for-houseplants/) covers the set-up.

Turning the pot a quarter turn every week or two also helps the plant grow evenly instead of leaning one way.

## Step two: prune back

Early spring, just before new growth starts, is the best time. The Missouri Botanical Garden describes cutting tall stems back to a side branch or bud; new shoots then appear from lower down, and healthy shoots should show within about three weeks. [Missouri Botanical Garden: renovating an indoor house plant](https://www.missouribotanicalgarden.org/gardens-gardening/your-garden/help-for-the-home-gardener/advice-tips-resources/visual-guides/renovating-an-indoor-house-plant)

How to cut:

1. Use clean, sharp scissors or secateurs.
2. Cut just above a leaf node, the point where a leaf joins the stem. Growth will come from the buds at or below that node. [University of Minnesota Extension: spring houseplant care](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/houseplants/spring-houseplant-care)
3. You do not need to cut everything at once. Shorten the longest stems first and leave some leaves on each stem so the plant can keep feeding itself.
4. Remove yellow or damaged leaves at the same time.

## Step three: root the cuttings

Many trailing and soft-stemmed plants, such as pothos, philodendron, tradescantia and many begonias, root easily from stem cuttings. Our guide to [propagating houseplants from cuttings](/guides/propagating-cuttings/) covers water and soil methods. Planting the rooted cuttings back into the same pot is a simple way to make a sparse plant look full again.

## Keep it compact from now on

Once the plant is in better light, a few habits keep it bushy:

- Turn the pot a quarter turn every week or two so all sides get light.
- In the growing season, trim the tip of a stem that is getting long, just above a node, rather than waiting for a big cut. [University of Minnesota Extension: spring houseplant care](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/houseplants/spring-houseplant-care)
- Feed only while the plant is actively growing and at or below the label rate; extra fertiliser in dim light does not replace light. See [feeding houseplants](/guides/fertilizing-houseplants/).
- Expect some stretch every winter and plan a light trim for early spring.

## Common questions

### Can I just stake a leggy plant?

You can tie it to a stake to stop it flopping, but that hides the problem rather than fixing it. Combine support with more light.

### Will the bare stem grow new leaves?

Sometimes. Many plants push new shoots from dormant buds below a cut. Bare stems on plants such as some succulents may not, so rooting the top is the better option there.

### When should I not prune?

Avoid hard pruning in the darkest part of winter, when growth is slow. Wait until days lengthen in early spring.
