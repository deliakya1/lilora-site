# An autumn reset for houseplants: a short checklist

As days shorten, houseplant care shifts down a gear. Bring summering plants back indoors before nights turn cold, check every plant for pests, give light-loving plants the best windows, and get ready to water and feed less. It takes an hour or two for a typical windowsill collection. The timing here is for the Northern Hemisphere, roughly September to November; in the Southern Hemisphere the same reset falls around March to May. [Illinois Extension: bring houseplants indoors before the first frost](https://extension.illinois.edu/news-releases/bring-houseplants-indoors-first-frost)

Most houseplants are tropical. Many are harmed by cool nights well before frost; Illinois Extension suggests moving them in when night temperatures fall to about 13 °C, and earlier for the most cold-sensitive tropicals, which can be damaged at around 10 °C. [Illinois Extension: bring houseplants indoors before the first frost](https://extension.illinois.edu/news-releases/bring-houseplants-indoors-first-frost)

Plants that spent summer in bright outdoor light will find a room much darker. Moving them gradually into shade for a week or two before bringing them in reduces leaf drop. [Illinois Extension: moving houseplants indoors](https://extension.illinois.edu/blogs/good-growing/2022-09-09-tips-moving-houseplants-indoors-and-overwinter-care)

## The checklist

1. **Inspect every plant.** Look under leaves, along stems and in leaf joints for insects, webbing or sticky honeydew. Check the surface of the mix and the drainage hole too. [University of Nebraska–Lincoln Extension: bringing houseplants indoors](https://lancaster.unl.edu/bringing-houseplants-indoors)
2. **Rinse and tidy.** Wash dust and stray insects off foliage with a gentle spray of water, remove dead leaves and clean pots and saucers.
3. **Keep newcomers apart.** Plants coming back from outdoors, and any new purchases, can stay in a separate spot for one to two weeks so pests have time to show. [University of Minnesota Extension: houseplant pests](https://extension.umn.edu/news/houseplant-pests)
4. **Reassign the windows.** Give the brightest spots to plants that need the most light, and move plants back from glass that will be cold at night.
5. **Clear the heat paths.** Check where radiators, heaters and vents are before the heating starts, and keep foliage out of their airflow.
6. **Note the change in routine.** Write down where each plant now stands, so later problems can be traced to a move.
7. **Plan to water less often.** Growth slows indoors in autumn, so plants generally need less water; keep checking the mix before each watering. [Illinois Extension: moving houseplants indoors](https://extension.illinois.edu/blogs/good-growing/2022-09-09-tips-moving-houseplants-indoors-and-overwinter-care)
8. **Wind down feeding.** Most houseplants are not fed through winter. [UConn Home and Garden Education Center: fertilizing houseplants](https://soiltesting.cahnr.uconn.edu/fertilizing-houseplants/)
9. **Leave repotting for spring,** unless a plant has a root problem that cannot wait.

## What to watch in the first weeks

A few yellow leaves after the move indoors are common while plants adjust to lower light. Watch for steady leaf loss, soft stems or pests that appear a week or two later. If leaves change colour, use [yellow houseplant leaves: what to check](/en/guides/yellow-houseplant-leaves/), and for anything that crawls, start with [common houseplant pests](/en/guides/houseplant-pests/).

Water carefully in this period. A plant in a dimmer room uses less water than it did outdoors, and pots no longer dry in the wind and sun. [Overwatered or underwatered?](/en/guides/overwatering-vs-underwatering/) explains how to tell.

## Climates that do not fit the calendar

If you live somewhere with mild winters, or in the tropics, the change may be small. The useful trigger is not a date but conditions: cooler nights, shorter days and the heating or air conditioning switching on. Brazil, Australia and South Africa have their autumn in March to May; parts of Brazil barely notice it. Use the checklist when your own season turns.

## Why write it down

An autumn reset changes several things at once: position, light, temperature and watering. If a plant struggles in November, a short record of what changed and when is the quickest way to find out why. In Lilora, a room change appears in the plant's history, and checks and waterings are separate records, so the autumn slowdown is visible rather than guessed. For the months ahead, see [winter houseplant care](/en/guides/winter-houseplant-care/).

## Common questions

### Should I repot plants when I bring them in?

Usually not. Autumn is a time of slowing growth; repot in spring unless roots are in trouble.

### Can I spray plants with insecticide before bringing them in?

Inspect and rinse first. Use a product only for a pest you have identified, labelled for that use, following the label.

### My plant dropped leaves after coming indoors. Is it dying?

Probably adjusting. Keep it in good light, avoid overwatering and watch new growth.
