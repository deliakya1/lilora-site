Do houseplants really purify indoor air?

The NASA study behind air-purifying plant lists used sealed chambers. What a 2019 review found about real homes, and what actually improves indoor air.

A short answer

Not in any way you would notice in a normal home. The idea comes from a 1989 NASA experiment in which plants removed chemicals from small sealed chambers. When researchers at Drexel University reviewed a dozen studies covering about 30 years, they calculated that you would need roughly 100 to 1,000 plants per square metre of floor to match the air cleaning that ordinary ventilation already provides. Opening windows and running extractor fans do far more for indoor air. Grow plants because you enjoy them, not as air purifiers. Drexel University: potted plants don't improve air quality

Where the claim comes from

In the late 1980s, NASA researchers looking for ways to keep air clean in space stations found that common houseplants could remove volatile organic compounds (VOCs), such as benzene and formaldehyde, from sealed test chambers. Drexel University: potted plants don't improve air quality Lists of "air-purifying plants", usually featuring spider plant, peace lily, dracaena and snake plant, trace back to that work. University of Illinois Extension: houseplants and indoor air quality

Interestingly, the research also found that much of the uptake happened in the roots and potting mix, through microorganisms, not only in the leaves. University of Illinois Extension: houseplants and indoor air quality

Why a sealed chamber is not a living room

A test chamber is small, closed and starts with a dose of one chemical. A home is large, air leaks in and out all the time, and new pollutants keep arriving from cooking, cleaning products and furniture. Iowa State University Extension puts it plainly: the highly controlled testing environment does not translate directly to everyday conditions, and houseplants have little effect on air quality in a home or office. Iowa State University Extension: do houseplants improve indoor air quality?

What the 2019 review found

Michael Waring and Bryan Cummings at Drexel University reviewed a dozen studies spanning about 30 years and published their analysis in the Journal of Exposure Science and Environmental Epidemiology. In every case, the rate at which plants cleaned the air was orders of magnitude slower than normal air exchange in buildings. To match a building's ventilation, or a couple of open windows in a house, they estimated 100 to 1,000 plants per square metre of floor space. Drexel University: potted plants don't improve air quality

Even an older estimate quoted by the University of Illinois, around 70 spider plants in 20 cm pots for an average house, came from sealed-chamber data and is far from a typical windowsill collection. University of Illinois Extension: houseplants and indoor air quality

What actually helps indoor air

  • Ventilate: open windows when you can, and use extractor fans when cooking or showering. Air exchange dilutes indoor pollutants much faster than plants. Drexel University: potted plants don't improve air quality
  • Reduce sources: store solvents and paints outside living areas and follow product directions.
  • For specific concerns, such as damp, mould or a gas appliance, follow local health and safety guidance rather than relying on plants.

Good reasons to keep plants anyway

None of this is a case against houseplants. Iowa State notes there are still many reasons to grow them: they beautify a home and let you garden even in the depth of winter. Iowa State University Extension: do houseplants improve indoor air quality? Choose plants for your light and your habits; our indoor plant light guide and how to choose a healthy houseplant are good places to start. If you have pets, check houseplants and pets too, since several plants on "air-purifying" lists are not safe for cats.

Common questions

Do plants add oxygen to a room?

They release oxygen in light and use some at night, but the amounts from a few pots are tiny compared with the air exchanged through a room. They do not replace ventilation.

Are some plants better than others at cleaning air?

Lab studies rank species differently, but at home the effect of any of them is negligible. Pick plants you can care for well.

Do plants raise humidity?

A little, through the water they release, but a few plants change room humidity very little. See humidity for houseplants.
